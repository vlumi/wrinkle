- **Below the limit MySQL halves the tail.** At 500/s the p99 is 86 to 116 ms on MySQL against 162 to 197 ms on Vitess, and the median 9 to 15 ms lower, in every shape. The night before gave the same: 79 to 88 ms against 158 to 180.
- **On Vitess, 1000/s is past the limit for three of four shapes.** Go, Java and the Rust gateway (in front of Go's services) had a p99 of 2.7 to 9 s at 1000/s in every repetition; the TypeScript monolith stayed under 1 s, as it has in every session. At 1500 and 2000/s every shape lost a third and half of its messages (62 to 78 % and 45 to 50 % delivered), whatever its language: the limit is Vitess's.
- **On MySQL, 1000/s held in 9 of 12 runs, and even 2000/s in three.** A run either stayed clean, as Java's at 2000/s with a p99 of 581 ms and Go's with 1.0 s, or stalled for 5 to 9 s, with the send round trip's p99 stalling with it, so the wait is in the services' writes, not in the change stream. Clean and stalled runs follow no order: the night before, the third repetitions were clean and the first two stalled, and here they are mixed. Over both nights, 7 of the 22 runs that count at 1000/s on MySQL stalled. What stalls the writes is not established; InnoDB's flushing under MySQL 8.4's default 100 MB redo log is the first thing to look at.
- **The TypeScript monolith tops out first on MySQL**: at 1500/s it delivered everything in every repetition, with its p99 at 10 to 12 s and its send round trip at 1.5 to 3.9 s at the median, where the Go and Java monoliths' runs either stayed clean or stalled outright. Its one thread is the likely limit, but its CPU per message falls as the rate rises, so that is not established.
- **The Vitess figures move between sessions.** Go's p99 at 1000/s was 0.5 s on 2026-10-24, 1.2 to 3.6 s the night before and 4.1 to 7.0 s here; Java's 0.4, 0.8 to 1.0 and 2.7 to 3.5 s. The Go and Java paths under load did not change between the last two sessions; at 1000/s Vitess is at its edge, where small differences in the machine's state show as seconds. Comparisons at 1000/s on Vitess hold only within a session.
- **CPU per message is lower on MySQL**: 21 % for the Go monolith, 8 % for Java, 20 % for TypeScript, and 11 % for the Rust gateway, which runs no SQL; so not all of it is the SQL path. The night before gave 21, 8, 22 and 9 %.

## History across the watermark

200 reads a second over 100 conversations, each a page of 50: the newest (all in MySQL), among the cold messages (all in HBase), and across the watermark (25 of each). p50 / p99 in ms. The mover's figure is messages moved per second of its rounds.

| Shape | Profile | Hot | Cold | Across | Mover |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Go | Vitess | 13.9 / 30.4 | 15.1 / 31.0 | 17.2 / 31.4 | 743 |
| | MySQL | 11.0 / 23.9 | 13.6 / 27.5 | 15.4 / 31.0 | 2081 |
| Java | Vitess | 18.3 / 32.0 | 20.1 / 40.6 | 21.7 / 43.7 | 1718 |
| | MySQL | 13.6 / 26.9 | 16.6 / 32.5 | 18.4 / 33.1 | 5773 |
| TypeScript | Vitess | 16.3 / 29.7 | 48.0 / 191.4 | 53.4 / 227.4 | 1577 |
| | MySQL | 15.1 / 24.3 | 55.9 / 238.4 | 62.5 / 244.5 | 4315 |

- **A cold page costs Go and Java 1 to 3 ms more than a hot one at the median**, and the page across the watermark, which reads both, 3 to 5 ms more. MySQL takes 1 to 5 ms off the hot reads and about as much off the others, which read MySQL first for the watermark.
- **The TypeScript backend's cold reads are three to four times slower**, 48 to 56 ms at the median and about 200 ms at p99: it reads HBase through the REST server, three HTTP requests a page (open a scanner, read it, delete it), where Go and Java read the region server directly. The night before, without the limit on requests in flight, its cold reads took 18 ms at the median while the mover was working through the backlog beside them, so the requests alone do not explain the difference; whether the REST server is slower after the mover has put a few hundred thousand rows through it (192 MB of heap in a 384 MB cap), or the limit queues the reads, is not established.
- **The Rust gateway's history runs** are not in the table: the protocol missed the mover in the Go services beside it, so its reads overlapped the mover's backlog (fixed after the session). Its reads were within 2.5 ms of the Go monolith's at the median.
- **The mover.** Before its fill, each history run waited for the mover to move what the earlier series left older than the 60 s window, 260,000 to 390,000 messages in 40 to 82 s on every shape. The fill's 20,000 messages then aged past the window over the 6 to 22 s they took to send, so each round moved about a second's worth per conversation and the figure measures the cost of those small moves. There Go's mover is 2.1 to 2.8 times slower than Java's and TypeScript's on both profiles, though it puts each conversation's messages in one batch like theirs; why is not established. On MySQL every mover is about three times faster than on Vitess, where the figure, summed over the conversation keyspace's two shards working in parallel, understates the wall-clock rate by up to half.

## Idle connections

The profile makes no difference: live memory at 500 and 4000 connections is within 6 MiB between the profiles for every shape (on Vitess: Go 20 and 116 MiB, Java 139 and 183, TypeScript 110 and 150), as it should, since idle connections do not touch the database. Resident memory, as on 2026-10-24, follows the password hashing of the sign-in phase in the monoliths.

## Reading it

- **Vitess's test server costs about half of the tail below the limit and half of the ceiling**, in every language, and the difference between the profiles is larger than any between the languages. It runs vtgate and one mysqld holding all four shards in one container, so this measures the prototype's Vitess rather than Vitess; a real topology is ROADMAP item 1 step 4. A deployment on a managed cloud's plain MySQL ([production.md](../production.md)) gives up the sharding, not this latency.
- **MySQL's ceiling is not yet a number**: about 1000 messages a second held every time and 2000 sometimes. The stalls decide it, and they are the next thing to explain, before any comparison of the backends above 1000/s on MySQL.
- **Below the limit the languages are level on both profiles**, as on 2026-10-24; at the limit, the TypeScript monolith holds longest on Vitess and gives out first on MySQL, which shows how each one queues rather than how fast it is.
