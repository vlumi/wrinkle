# Six shapes after coalesced acks and batched fan-out, 2026-10-21

> Followed by [2026-10-24](2026-10-24-shapes.md), with the TypeScript backend.

The protocol in [measuring.md](../measuring.md), now with a 1500/s step, every series three times, each backend from a freshly created stack (`FRESH=1`), in one session on one machine (`REPS=3 make measure-all`, 12:39 to 16:39 UTC). Medians of the three repetitions throughout. Raw runs: [go](2026-10-21-go.jsonl), [go-gateway](2026-10-21-go-gateway.jsonl), [java](2026-10-21-java.jsonl), [java-gateway](2026-10-21-java-gateway.jsonl), [elixir](2026-10-21-elixir.jsonl), [rust](2026-10-21-rust.jsonl).

- Commit: 607dcfd. Since [2026-10-15](2026-10-15-shapes.md): every gateway writes a connection's acks at most once a minute, and on close; the Go and Java fan-out store a message's copies in one transaction per user-keyspace shard with whatever else is queued for it; Go's send path remembers a direct conversation's members, as Java's already did. The Go and Java send paths are now alike.
- Monoliths: Go 1.27.2 and OpenJDK 25.0.4.1, one process each with all roles.
- Gateways: the Go and Java backends split into a gateway process and a services process over the internal RPC; the Elixir gateway (Elixir 1.20.4, OTP 29, Bandit) and the Rust gateway (Rust 1.99.0, tokio, axum) beside a Go services process. The measured process is the gateway.
- Machine: MacBook Pro (Mac16,5), Apple M4 Max (16 cores), 48 GB, macOS 26.7.1; Docker Desktop 4.96.0 (engine 29.9.1) with its default 16 CPUs and 8 GB.
- Infrastructure: `infra/docker-compose.yml` as committed, unchanged since 2026-10-15.
- Every run connected all its devices and every received message decrypted.

- **The highest sustained step is 1000/s for five shapes**, twice what it was: every message delivered in every repetition, the median p99 under 1 s. At 1500/s every shape saturated (53 to 61 % delivered, sends timing out), so the database's limit now lies between 1000 and 1500 messages a second; it was not refined further.
- **At 1000/s the tails vary between repetitions.** One repetition each of the Go and Java gateways had a p99 of 6.7 s and 2.6 s, delivering everything all the same, so the medians come from the other two. The step is close to the limit.
- **The Elixir gateway sustained 500/s, not 1000/s.** At 1000/s about 5 % of its sends timed out (the load generator's 10 s deadline) and the send round trip rose to 2.2 s at the median, while the gateway used about 0.4 of a core. The other gateways in front of the same Go services delivered everything, so the queueing is in the Elixir gateway; where, the gRPC client's connection, Bandit or a single process on the path, is not established.
- **Below the limit every shape is faster than on 2026-10-15**, 63 to 72 ms p50 at 500/s against 86 to 118, with p99 at 123 to 179 ms against 189 to 268. The database does less per message, so each statement waits less.

The gateways' saving is the acks: the load generator acks once a second per device, and each ack used to be an RPC to the services, now one a minute. The monoliths save that and the fan-out's statements as well. The monoliths' CPU includes the services and the fan-out and the gateways' does not, so the two groups do not compare on CPU.

## Reading it

- **The database is still the limit, but at twice the rate.** In a diagnostic count at 500/s (Go monolith), ack writes are down to 0.1 per message, and batching took the fan-out's statements from about 12 per message to 2 and the total from 24 to 13 (Java's is 12.7); the copies share transactions about six at a time at 500/s, more as the load grows. What remains is mostly the send transaction, about ten statements.
- **Rust stays the lean gateway on every axis**: 201 ms of CPU per thousand messages, 31 MiB under load, the lowest latency of the gateways at every rate, level with the Go gateway at 100/s.
- **The monoliths are the fastest shapes below the limit**, 63 and 67 ms p50 at 500/s, since they skip the internal RPC.
- **Elixir's gap moved.** On 2026-10-15 it was 23 ms slower at 500/s and level at 100/s; now it is level at 500/s and fails at 1000/s, the first step where its gateway, rather than the database, is the limit.
