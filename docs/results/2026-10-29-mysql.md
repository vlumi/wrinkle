## Reading it

- **The ceiling on this machine is Vitess's test server.** With the durability matched, plain MySQL carried every shape at 2000 messages a second, twice what any shape has held on Vitess, and Vitess failed at 1000/s for three shapes of four, as before. That is vtgate and the four shards in one container on a laptop, not Vitess in a real topology (ROADMAP item 1 step 4), but it means the prototype's comparisons of the backends above 1000/s need the MySQL profile.
- **Below it, MySQL more than halves the tail and takes up to a quarter off the CPU per message**, in every language; the difference between the profiles is still larger than any between the languages.
- **The MySQL figures are without durability**, as Vitess's always were. A deployment keeps the fsync at each commit, on disks that are not a virtual machine's; on Docker Desktop's, with it, the runs stalled from 1000/s ([2026-10-28](2026-10-28-mysql.md#the-stalls-explained-2026-10-29)).
- **Open:** the MySQL profile's ceiling, above 2000/s; the TypeScript backend's cold reads through the REST server, still twice Go's and Java's after the fix below; Go's slower mover.

## The TypeScript backend's cold reads, explained (2026-10-30)

HBase's REST server ran with 192 MB of heap in a 384 MB cap, and Docker lets a container use as much swap again as its cap. Under reads it needed more. Its HBase client starts up to 256 threads (200 were running), Jetty 100, and besides the heap the JVM took about 90 MB of its own and about 220 MB through malloc. So it paged its heap, and every collection and every request that touched a paged-out page waited on Docker Desktop's disk.

On a fresh Vitess stack at eebebab, the history series alone for the TypeScript monolith, three times, with the server sampled every 2 s: its cold reads took 78, 69 and 49 ms at the median and 313, 631 and 228 ms at p99. Through the three runs the server sat at its cap with up to 308 MB in swap and swapped in 1.6 million pages, about 6 GB. Without swap, at 384 and at 512 MB, the kernel killed it at the first reads. How the third run on Vitess failed the night before is not established, since the server's logs went with its container, but it ran in the same cap with swap.

Its client now runs at most 32 threads, the TypeScript backend's own limit on requests in flight, and the server has 768 MB and no swap (`infra/docker-compose.yml`, `infra/hbase/hbase-site.xml`). Under the same three runs it peaked at 672 MB, never reached its cap, and read cold pages in 34, 34 and 35 ms at the median and 112, 68 and 51 ms at p99, every read a full page. That is still about twice Go's and Java's, which read the region server directly; the rest is the REST server's three HTTP requests per page.
